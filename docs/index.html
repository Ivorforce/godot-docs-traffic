Which parts of the Godot manual carry the reading, and which cost more to maintain than their readership suggests. Page traffic is joined against the godot-docs source tree, so page length and edit history sit next to the traffic. It is a map for people who maintain the docs — not a scoreboard, and not a list of pages to delete.

Attention against footprint

For each part of the manual, its share of all reading against its share of the manual's size. Hover a bar for the counts and for pageviews per thousand words. The two bars are shares of different totals, so read the gap as a ratio: a section at half its share of reading is read half as much per word as the manual overall.

Where the grey bar runs past the blue one, that part is larger than its readership — a question worth asking, not an answer. Reference material is supposed to be consulted rarely, and a section can be long because the subject is hard.

Footprint is words by default, roughly what a section costs to keep correct, since review, updating and translation all scale with prose. Pages answers a different question — how many separate documents a reader moves between — and the two disagree. The class reference is excluded unless the path bar's first entries put it back.

What each page costs and returns

Every page in the current section: how much it is read (horizontal) against how long it is (vertical), both on log scales, with the crosshairs at this section's medians rather than at invented thresholds. Pages low and to the right are read a lot and say little; pages high and to the left are long and seldom read — the ones worth looking at, since length is roughly what a page costs to keep correct. Hover a point for the page; click to filter the table below to it.

Colour is lines changed over the churn window (…) against the page's length now — whether anyone is already working on it, the same measure that shades the grid further down. Pale is the quiet end: a page high, left and pale is long, seldom read, and nobody has been near it in two years. The same page in dark blue is somebody else's project already, and the useful thing to do about it is nothing.

A quiet page may be finished rather than forgotten; nothing here separates those. Short pages swing hardest, so read the colour of a small point as the coarse signal it is. When the path bar puts the class reference back, the colours mean something else again: those pages sync in bulk from the engine's source, so their turnover is the size of whatever the engine changed.

Where the docs are being written

Lines changed by quarter over two years (…) — added and deleted counted together, from the git history of the docs source. Figures are rounded (24k, 1.3k); hover any cell for the exact one. Rows follow the path bar, the same as the charts above, so drilling into a section replaces them with what is inside it. Empty cells are the reason it is here: a part of the docs untouched for a quarter is the one thing the pull request feed does not show.

The shading is those lines against how long the row is now, so it says something the figures do not. In the first half of 2023 getting_started/ changed 3,531 lines against tutorials/'s 24,229 and was the harder worked of the two, turning over 45% of its own length where tutorials turned over 23%. The darkest cell is the most intensively changed one currently shown, not a fixed mark, so the colours redraw when you change section; the numbers do not.

Columns are calendar quarters, so a closed one never changes again and two versions of this report can be compared. A rule marks where each year begins. The last column, marked *, stops at the docs commit rather than at the end of its quarter, so a fall at the right edge may be nothing but that.

The final column is the row's whole window, and the bar beside it draws added against deleted from the point where the two balance: right for a section that came out longer, left for one that came out shorter. A bar filling its half was only written or only removed; a stub either side is a section reworked at the length it already had. Read red as shorter and not as worse — trimming and merging are among the things this report was built to find. Pages deleted outright are not in this table, so every bar sits a little further right than the docs went.

Getting started, tutorial by tutorial

The four tutorials of the getting-started guide, in the order the docs present them, each page measured against its own tutorial's landing page. Order and grouping come from the table of contents, so a restructure moves this chart instead of breaking it. Hover a point for the page and the count behind its percentage. The guide is a third of all reading in the hand-written manual. This section and the next are fixed — the path bar does not reach them.

The percentages are not conversion, and the landing page is a denominator rather than a door: readers arrive from search at any step and leave whenever they have what they came for, so a step above 100% means people entered there. The trend across a tutorial is the reliable part; the gap between two neighbouring points is not, since a few hundred pageviews either way is a visible part of the line.

One departure is too large to read that way. signals.html, the last step of step_by_step, draws more than the two steps before it — 14,397 pageviews against 8,660 and 5,987. Direct arrival probably explains it: a concept people come looking for by name rather than one they reach by working forward. It is the one page in the guide worth treating as an entrance in its own right.

Removed pages with no redirect

Pages that were removed from the manual, still receive traffic, and are not forwarded anywhere — so these readers land on the “page not found” page and have to start over. This list is what _static/redirects.csv does not yet cover; most removed pages are forwarded by it and are left out. A date inside the reporting period means the page was readable for part of it, so its count is partly ordinary reading rather than missed arrivals.

Read the Docs applies its own redirects before the page renders, so those never reach this report at all — absence from this table means forwarded or never removed, not unmeasured. The redirects file is read at the docs commit shown at the top of the page.

Caveats
  • Everything here is pageviews. Per-visitor and per-session figures — unique visitors, bounce rate, session duration — are not reliable on this analytics setup and are used nowhere. It offers neither scroll depth nor time on page, so there is no engagement signal at all: nothing here measures whether a page was useful, only how often it was opened.
  • Analytics run on JavaScript. Bots, LLM crawlers, readers with scripts blocked, and everyone reading the docs offline or in the editor's built-in help are invisible — and the docs are read in all of those ways.
  • A URL counts as a page only if it resolves to a source file in godot-docs at the commit shown above. That drops removed pages, Sphinx's search page and scanner noise — about 2% of raw pageviews, accounted for under "removed pages with no redirect" so the loss is visible rather than silent. Roughly half of it is readers being successfully forwarded by the docs' own redirects.
  • Low traffic is not low value. A page read a few hundred times a month may be the one that unblocks a contributor, or matters precisely because it is rarely needed. Every page of the manual was read at least once this period — there is no dead weight to find here, only proportion. Every chart proposes candidates for a human to look at, and none is a recommendation to delete anything.
  • Word counts and edit dates come from the docs source tree as it is today, not as it was during the traffic window. A recent edit date is also no sign a page was reviewed: most are typo corrections and link sweeps, which is why nothing here is sorted or ranked by date.
  • Lines changed is a measure of activity, not of quality or of need. A page nobody has touched reads the same whether it is correct and finished or forgotten, and nothing in this data separates those two. Read it as a map of where work happened, never as a list of what to work on next.
  • The churn window ends at the docs commit shown above, not at the end of the traffic window, so the newest churn column is a part-period and the two spans should not be compared period for period. Churn also reaches back two years where traffic reaches back thirteen months.
  • The class reference is generated from the engine's source and synced in bulk, so its git dates record sync commits rather than anyone writing. For that reason the report opens on the manual without it; the path bar's first entries count its 1,079 pages in, or open the report on them alone.
  • Only English /en/stable/ pages are counted in detail. Translated reading is summed across all other languages per page. Both sides count the current docs only — pinned releases like /en/4.7/ and /ja/4.5/ are excluded, a sixth of translated reading, named in the tile above.
