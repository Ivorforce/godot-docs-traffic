Which parts of the Godot manual carry the reading, and which cost more to maintain than their readership suggests. Built by joining page traffic against the godot-docs source tree, so page length and last-edit date sit next to the traffic rather than traffic standing alone. It is a map for people who maintain the docs — not a scoreboard, and not a list of pages to delete.

The whole English /en/stable/ manual over …. Everything from the path bar down to the page table follows the section you select there, and the sections after it are fixed. These totals follow only the path bar's first choice, of whether the class reference counts as part of the manual.

Attention against footprint

For each part of the manual, the share of reading it receives against the share of the manual it occupies. Use the path bar above to move between sections; hover a bar for the underlying counts and for pageviews per thousand words. Where the grey bar runs past the blue one, that part is larger than its readership — a question worth asking, not an answer: reference material is supposed to be consulted rarely, and a section can be long because the subject is hard.

The two bars are percentages of different totals — one of all pageviews, one of all the manual's words — so the distance between them is a ratio, not a subtraction. A section at half its share of reading is read half as much per word as the manual overall, whether the bars read 4% and 8% or 40% and 80%.

Footprint is measured in words by default, because that is roughly what a section costs to keep correct — review, updating and translation all scale with prose, not with file count. Page count answers a different question, about how many separate documents a reader has to move between, and the two disagree: a section of many short pages looks large by page count and modest by words, and a section of a few long ones the other way round. Across the manual, pages range from under 150 words to over 2,000. The class reference is excluded by default from both, being generated from the engine's own source rather than written or trimmed here.

What each page costs and returns

Every page in the current section: how much it is read (horizontal) against how long it is (vertical), both on log scales, with the crosshairs at this section's medians rather than at invented thresholds. Pages low and to the right are read a lot and say little; pages high and to the left are long and seldom read — the ones worth looking at, since length is roughly what a page costs to keep correct. Hover a point for the page; click to filter the table below to it.

Colour finishes the question the position starts: whether anyone is already working on it — lines changed over the churn window (…) against the page's length now, the same measure that shades the grid further down. Read the pale end as the quiet end. A page high and to the left and pale is the one to look at: long, seldom read, and nobody has been near it in two years. The same page in dark blue is somebody else's project already, and the useful thing to do about it is nothing.

In the long-and-seldom-read corner the split is roughly even — of 103 pages there, 17 are barely touched and 41 lightly worked, against 24 reworked and 21 rewritten outright. That is the whole use of the colour: the position alone would have handed over all 103.

Colour was chosen for what it is not: it does not repeat the axes. Turnover measures −0.09 against reading and 0.01 against length across this manual, so a point's colour is genuinely a third fact about it. Month-on-month change and translation share were both tried here and both leaned on the horizontal axis — translation share slides from 0.95× in the least-read tenth of pages to about 0.45× in the most-read, which paints the traffic distribution rather than the page. Both remain in the table below, where they are read one page at a time and that ceases to matter.

Short pages swing, and the ratio has no ceiling: a 22-line stub can take 175 lines of work and read as 7.95×, where a 500-line page would need a rewrite half as large again to pass 1×. They crowd both ends accordingly: 38% of the barely-touched sit in the shortest quarter of the manual, where chance would put 25%. Read the colour of a small point as the coarse signal it is. A page can also be quiet because it is finished — the measure records that nobody has needed to change it, and cannot say whether that is because it is right or because nobody looked. When the path bar puts the class reference back, the colours mean something else again: those pages are synced in bulk from the engine's source, so their turnover is the size of whatever the engine changed.

Where the docs are being written

Lines changed by quarter over two years (…) — added and deleted counted together, from the git history of the docs source. Figures are rounded (24k, 1.3k); hover any cell for the exact one. Rows follow the path bar, the same as the charts above: at the top level they are the parts of the manual, and drilling into one replaces them with what is inside it, so a section's quiet corners can be found the same way its busy ones can. This is a record of where effort went, not a ranking: it says what was worked on, and cannot say what should have been. Empty cells are the reason it is here — a part of the docs untouched for a quarter is worth knowing about, and it is the one thing here the pull request feed does not show.

The figures are lines; the shading is those lines measured against how long that row is now, so it says something the figures do not. In the first half of 2023 getting_started/ changed 3,531 lines against tutorials/'s 24,229 and was the harder worked of the two, turning over 45% of its own length where tutorials turned over 23% of its. Read the colour in both directions; read the figures for how much work that actually was.

The darkest cell is the most intensively changed one currently shown, not a fixed mark, and the ramp is a square root. Sections almost never change by their whole length in a quarter, and the cells are heavily lopsided at every level — the busiest is typically thirteen times a middling one — so a scale pinned to either end left nearly every cell in the palest tenth of it. Because the top of the scale moves with what is on screen, the colours redraw when you change section; the numbers do not. Dividing by the length today rather than the length at the time is deliberate: one divisor for a whole row leaves its profile intact, where a moving one would bend the shape the row exists to show.

Columns are calendar quarters, not the last eight counted back from today, so a closed column never changes again and two versions of this report can be compared. The price is the last column, marked *: it stops at the docs commit rather than at the end of its quarter, so it is short by however much of the period is still to come and a fall at the right edge may be nothing but that. Quarters rather than months because months were too fine — a third of the cells were empty and one piece of work spread across two or three of them, so an empty cell recorded the rhythm of when pull requests land rather than anything about the page.

Measured in lines rather than commits, because a commit counts a typo fix and a rewrite alike: the most-committed page of the last year took 13 commits, while the single largest change was 1,822 lines in three of them. Renames are followed to where a file ended up, so a page that moved keeps one history instead of two halves filed under two names — 846 of them across this manual's history, without which the 2022 restructure would read as whole sections deleting themselves into others. Two years is a choice about what anyone can act on rather than a limit of the data: four years was tried, reaches back some eight minor releases, and where a level has more rows than fit it filled half the list with pages last worked on in 2023.

The added and deleted columns are the same work seen a second way. A section adding several times what it deletes is growing new material; one where the two are close was reworked at its existing length, which is a different activity and often a more careful one. The class reference follows the same choice as everywhere else on this page — left out until the path bar's first entry puts it back — but read its rows differently when it is in: those pages are generated from the engine's source and synced in bulk, so their diffs are the size of whatever the engine changed rather than of anything anyone wrote. They are three quarters of all edited lines.

Getting started, tutorial by tutorial

The sections from here on are fixed: the path bar above does not reach them, because each answers a question about a named part of the site rather than about whichever part is selected.

The four tutorials of the getting-started guide, in the order the docs present them, each page measured against its own tutorial's landing page. The order and the grouping are read from the table of contents, so a restructure moves this chart instead of breaking it. Hover a point for the page and the count behind its percentage. The guide is a third of all reading in the hand-written manual, a quarter once the class reference is counted in.

Each tutorial is drawn against its own landing page because the four are far apart in absolute reading — the 2D game is roughly three times the 3D one — and on a shared scale the smaller two flatten into the axis with their shape still in them. The percentages are not conversion, and the landing page is a denominator rather than a door: readers arrive from search at any step and leave whenever they have what they came for. A step above 100% would mean people entered there, not that readers multiplied.

All four decline broadly in the order the docs set, but the step-to-step comparisons are looser than the overall shape and should be read that way. The 3D game's later steps sit between 1,400 and 2,500 pageviews, where a few hundred either way is a visible part of the line, and small recoveries at step 3 and step 5 appear in both game tutorials without meaning much. The trend across a tutorial is the reliable part; the gap between two neighbouring points is not.

One departure is too large to read that way. signals.html, the last step of step_by_step, draws more than the two steps before it — 14,397 pageviews against 8,660 and 5,987. Position cannot explain that and direct arrival probably can: it is a concept people come looking for by name rather than one they reach by working forward. It is the only page in the guide whose reading the sequence does not account for, which makes it the one worth treating as an entrance in its own right.

Removed pages with no redirect

Pages that were removed from the manual, still receive traffic, and are not forwarded anywhere — so these readers land on the “page not found” page and have to start over. Most removed pages are forwarded, by _static/redirects.csv, and those are left out of this table: their readers arrive where they meant to. This list is what the redirects file does not yet cover. A date inside the reporting period means the page was readable for part of it, so its count is partly ordinary reading rather than missed arrivals.

Read the Docs applies its own redirects before the page renders, so those never reach this report at all — which is why absence from this table means forwarded or never removed, not unmeasured. The redirects file is read at the docs commit shown at the top of the page; entries added since then will still appear here.

Caveats
  • Everything here is pageviews. Per-visitor and per-session figures — unique visitors, bounce rate, session duration — are not reliable on this analytics setup and are used nowhere. The same setup offers neither scroll depth nor time on page, so there is no engagement signal available at all: nothing here claims to measure whether a page was useful, only how often it was opened.
  • Analytics run on JavaScript. Bots, LLM crawlers, readers with scripts blocked, and everyone reading the docs offline or in the editor's built-in help are invisible. Counts are humans-with-JavaScript only, and the docs are read in all of those other ways.
  • A URL counts as a page only if it resolves to a source file in godot-docs at the commit shown above. That drops removed pages, Sphinx's search page and scanner noise — about 2% of raw pageviews, accounted for under "removed pages with no redirect" so the loss is visible rather than silent. Roughly half of it is readers being successfully forwarded by the docs' own redirects, which record a pageview at the old URL before sending them on.
  • Low traffic is not low value. A page read a few hundred times a month may be the one that unblocks a contributor, documents the thing nobody else wrote down, or matters precisely because it is rarely needed. Every page of the manual was read at least once this period — there is no dead weight to find here, only proportion. Every chart on this page proposes candidates for a human to look at, and none of them is a recommendation to delete anything.
  • Word count and last-edit date come from the docs source tree as it is today, not as it was during the traffic window. A page rewritten last week shows last week's date against a month of traffic.
  • A recent last edit does not mean a page was reviewed. Of the 130 manual pages last edited in the three months to the commit above, the median one was last touched by a commit changing under five lines, and 30% by one changing two or fewer — typo corrections and link sweeps. An old date is no clearer, since a page can be untouched because it is still correct. The column is a lookup for a single page, which is why nothing on this page is sorted, coloured or ranked by it.
  • Lines changed is a measure of activity, not of quality or of need. It counts what was edited, so a page nobody has touched reads the same whether it is correct and finished or forgotten — and nothing in this data separates those two. It also has no view on whether the edit was an improvement. Read it as a map of where work happened, never as a list of what to work on next.
  • The churn window ends at the docs commit shown at the top of the page, which is not the end of the traffic window: the source tree is read when the inventory runs and the traffic when the fetch does, so the newest churn column is a part-period and may lag the newest traffic month by weeks. The two spans are not aligned and should not be compared period for period. Churn also reaches back two years where traffic reaches back thirteen months, so the older half of the grid has no traffic figure beside it at all.
  • The class reference is generated from the engine's source and synced in bulk, so its git dates record sync commits rather than anyone writing. For that reason the report opens on the manual without it; the first entry in the path bar puts its 1,079 pages back in.
  • Only English /en/stable/ pages are shown in detail. Translation reads are summed across all other languages per page, and both sides count the current docs only, so the share compares like with like. That takes some care, because the languages do not name their versions alike: English has stable and latest, both English-only, while the translations have neither and the edition their language switcher points at is 4.x. Pinned releases (/en/4.7/, /ja/4.5/ and older) are excluded on both sides — 130,121 pageviews of translated reading, a sixth of it, named in the tile above and in no share on this page.
